
1. Data Alignment :
      The data_alignment step aligns Hindi and Telugu subtitle sentences so that each line in Hindi matches its correct Telugu translation

      It ensures both languages have parallel, sentence-by-sentence pairs for later processing like tokenization or similarity scoring.

steps :
  1. Load Subtitles 
       Hindi and Telugu .srt files are read and parsed using parse_srt().
  2. Time-Based Alignment 
       Sentences are matched if their subtitle timestamps overlap by more than a threshold (e.g., 50%).
       This ensures both sentences refer to the same moment in the video.
  3. Length-Based Refinement
       Among time-aligned pairs, remove mismatches where text lengths differ too much (e.g., one line is much longer than the other).
  4. DTW (Dynamic Time Warping) Alignment
       For remaining unaligned subtitles, DTW compares the length patterns of sentences
       It “stretches or compresses” sequences to find the best matching pairs, even if timing doesn’t align perfectly.
  5. Scoring and Final Selection
      Each pair is given a score based on both time overlap and text-length similarity.
      The best, non-redundant pairs (each subtitle used only once) are chosen.

2. Data Tokenization 
   Convert aligned Hindi-Telugu sentence pairs into subword tokens for NLP tasks, using Byte-Pair Encoding (BPE).

steps :
 1. Collect all sentences from data_aligned TSV files (Hindi and Telugu together).
 2. Train separate BPE tokenizers for Hindi and Telugu:
      . Count word frequencies.
      . Split words into characters initially.
      . Iteratively merge the most frequent adjacent pairs until the vocabulary reaches the desired size.
      . Store all merges for later tokenization.
 3. Tokenize each sentence using the trained merges:
      . Frequent sequences become single tokens.
      . Rare words stay split into smaller subwords.
 4. Save tokenized sentences into TSV files 


3. Data similarity scoring 
   After tokenizing, we have aligned Hindi-Telugu sentences.
   The goal of data_similarity_scoring is to measure how similar each Hindi sentence is to its corresponding Telugu sentence.
   This can help detect misalignments or low-quality translations.

  1. Normalize text:
      Remove extra spaces and diacritics from each sentence.
  2. Extract features for similarity:
     means converting into vector for next similarity score calculation 
     . Character-level n-grams (1–3 chars).
     . Structural n-grams (2–5 Unicode category codes, capturing the pattern of letters, spaces, punctuation).
     . Length similarity (based on sentence lengths).
  3. Calculate similarity scores for each Hindi-Telugu pair:
     . Character similarity → cosine similarity of char n-gram TF-IDF vectors.
     . Structural similarity → cosine similarity of structural n-gram TF-IDF vectors.
     . Length similarity → 1 − relative difference in sentence lengths.
     . Combine these with weights: 0.5 (char) + 0.3 (structure) + 0.2 (length).
  4. Process tokenized files:
     . Load each TSV file from data_tokenized.
     . Compute similarity for each aligned sentence pair.
     . Save results in a new TSV in data_similarity_scoring with columns:
       Hindi    Telugu    Similarity_Score
